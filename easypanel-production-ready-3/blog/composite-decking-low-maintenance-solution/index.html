Low-Maintenance Composite Decking: An Upkeep Checklist

Composite deck boards generally skip the regular oiling and staining associated with timber decking. Most of the work is keeping the surface clean and making sure water can drain. How much cleaning you’ll do depends on what falls onto the deck and how quickly it dries. A shaded deck under trees usually needs more attention than a covered deck with good airflow.

When to check your composite deck

  • After heavy rain or windy weather. Sweep off leaves and clear surface debris caught between boards. If water keeps pooling in the same spot after the surrounding deck has dried, check whether something is blocking drainage. Ask your installer to review persistent pooling.
  • After cooking or entertaining. Wipe up food, oil and drink spills promptly. Use a drip tray under the barbecue and check that any protective mat is suitable for your boards and won’t trap moisture.
  • When surfaces start to look dirty. Follow the board supplier’s care instructions. Start with the gentlest approved method, such as a soft brush and mild soapy water if permitted, and rinse away the residue. See the step-by-step cleaning guide for more detail.
  • When you move pots or furniture. Clear out leaf litter and dirt underneath. Use suitable furniture pads and lift heavy items when moving them. Check for recurring damp patches under pots or mats.

What to check before you buy

Look at where leaves, runoff and barbecue grease will land. Ask the supplier for the board’s care instructions and warranty terms, including advice on cleaners, mats and pressure washing. If you’re choosing a finish, look at a sample in the sun and shade where the deck will sit.

Plan drainage and access for cleaning as part of the installation. Use the instructions for your chosen board and fixing system for gaps, clearances and ventilation. Our decking installation guide covers what to check when planning your layout.

Does composite decking need oiling or sealing?

Composite boards generally don’t need routine oiling or sealing. Check the supplier’s instructions before applying any coating or stain remover. If you’re deciding which material to buy, see our composite versus timber comparison for the upfront and ongoing trade-offs.
